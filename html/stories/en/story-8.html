The Little Sheep and the Wolves of the Moon

This story aims to strengthen family, which is far more than a simple unit in society — it is the shared purpose born of love that gives meaning to every person, home, nation, continent, and to the world in how they relate to one another. Animal psychology: calm, gentle; said of people who, despite having character and a will of their own, stay positive, avoid violence, and are entirely without aggression.

Once upon a time… there was a lovely little sheep who loved it when the end of the week arrived, so she could run through the meadow with her little brothers, cousins, and the rest of the family, alongside her clever owner.

This little lamb was quite mischievous — she ate every plant she found along the way, and when she wanted to drink water she never went to the trough like the rest of the flock; instead she trotted briskly to the lake, always making the mistake of, between sips, snapping up a little fish into her mouth.

She always did the same thing: she'd wade into the lake, and once her mouth was full with some little fish, she'd set it down in the meadow, threading it onto one of her little horns, which grew a bit more every day, and then continue on her way.

Happily she'd return to the lake, drink a little more water, and hop, excited, all the way to the house of the farm's owners where she lived, leaving the little fish, ready to eat, in the kitchen basket, having decorated her little head with it quite charmingly.

The little lamb had an owner who ran to her side now and then, on weekends. The rest of the week this boy studied in the city at an arts academy; arriving home on weekends, he felt great satisfaction feeding and caring for his animals, both the domestic ones and some wild animals who occasionally joined the group — but he always paid special attention to the little lamb, "his favorite."

One day, worried about the little sheep he had pampered since she was small, always wanting to protect her, he asked his father: —Father, you take very good care of my little sheep and the rest of my pets, but I worry that something bad might happen to her while I'm away — I don't want her to get lost, or ever lose her way home.

The loving father, with a nod of his face, drew a "Yes" that let him feel reassured: —Don't worry, son! he answered. I will always find room to look after your little lamb!

Trusting him, the boy went to sleep, convinced his father would take good care of her in his absence.

The next day, before the bus came to take the boy to school, he went to his favorite pet and, stroking her freshly shed wool, told her: —"My little fluff", I'm going away for a few days, but please don't wander past the lake that marks our territory — it could be dangerous. That's the den of some wild wolves.

Not quite understanding, she stayed very still while he told her: —If, some time, out of mischief, a wolf ever comes near you, don't look it in the eyes — pass yourself off as just another member of its pack, without letting it know you're a lamb, until you can find your way back home.

…A week went by, and then another; the boy stayed in the city for a painting festival, and everyone went along to support this young artist competing to be the best in his school.

Meanwhile, the farm was left alone, everything in order, but… the little lamb, together with three of her little brothers, went wandering beyond the lake, into the territory where the merciless, cruel wolves lived, wolves well used to causing great harm.

The little lambs were frightened when they saw them; the little lamb remembered her owner's words and warned the others. All of them, quite cunningly, lowered their heads and spoke with the wolves, but never looked them in the face, so they wouldn't discover they were a very different kind of animal.

The wolves and these little lambs stayed together for three days and two nights; their heads already ached from staring at the ground, but they held firm to their plan of denying who they truly were.

They knew that the slightest slip could land them in the belly of one of these great beasts.

When, on the third day, the full moon rose and the pack of wolves gathered to sing to "Mother Nature," right at midnight, when they all raised their heads to let out that long, mournful howl every carnivorous mammal owes the moon since birth, the little lambs ran as fast as they could, until they reached the land where they had always felt safe, loved, and protected.

They managed to escape; on arriving, they collapsed to the ground, trembling with exhaustion, afraid of being found out, but happy and excited to be somewhere they no longer had to hide their faces, for they were loved, needed, precisely for who they were.

As the little lambs remembered the long howl the wolves had sung to the moon, they looked at one another, laughing, preferring to be cheerful and calm, carrying this hard experience with them, but feeling saved, laughing, faces turned to the Sun.

And they say that ever since, while some hide, out of wickedness, in the thick darkness, others defy life, looking lovingly toward the sunrise.

That is why… we must always guard our souls to stay in the truth, listening to the counsel of whoever cares for us and walks beside us, so that nothing keeps us from staying joyful, offering our smile to the Sun.

Ah… and may you, too, wake each day and stay innocent — walking joyfully beneath the morning light!
