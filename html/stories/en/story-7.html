The Bee in Love with the Songbird

This story aims to strengthen love, life's natural balance — a reality that outlasts any simple human feeling, and rises above disciplines, differences, and even human doctrines. Animal psychology: altruistic, hard-working, loyal, patient; said of people who talk long, lively, and animated (much like the buzz this animal gives off).

Once upon a time… there was a bee madly in love with a little songbird who lived singing just outside the hive.

Every evening the bee watched him, mischievous and beaming, for without meaning to, he had stolen her little heart.

The rest of the bees, hard at work building a great honeycomb to please their Queen, grew puzzled seeing that, at every sunset, the mysterious little bee slipped away quietly, and no one could guess where her little wings were carrying her.

One day the Queen Bee ordered a great feast prepared, where every bee had to show off a beautiful smile to cheer the kingdom, lift everyone's spirits, and gather fresh strength for the new day's work.

They dressed up the honeycomb with great care, arranging red and white rose petals around it; they added bunches of gardenias, the occasional poppy, not to mention the daisies and jasmines each Bee wore on her tail. The male bees and the drone, as soon as they saw the bees getting dressed, cut little bellflowers and set them on their heads, which made them look elegant and very, very manly.

All to delight the Queen Bee! The party went exactly as planned; as soon as the Queen Bee appeared, every single bee in the hive paraded past — except the lovesick little bee.

The little bee, lost in love and mad with passion for the songbird, couldn't resist doing what she did every evening, and slipped away, hidden, to the waterfall where the bird sang the melody that lulled the Sun to sleep.

The songbird, seeing her…. felt his heart leap, for he too longed for the hour he'd meet his cheerful, tender little bee.

The little songbird sang, and the more melodious and loud his song grew, the deeper the feeling it stirred in the little bee.

In a buzz, through tears, she told him: —I love you, I love you! —I love you too, I have always needed you! he answered her.

They looked at each other for a while, he silent, until she spoke again: —My Queen planned a great gathering to lift everyone's spirits, and I haven't managed to obey her; this makes me feel sad, but I believe being with you is where I should be — yet no bee has ever disobeyed her Queen, and…

He couldn't resist the mood of the moment, and began singing to the stars despite having already lulled the Sun to sleep, interrupting her to say: —Tell me, don't you feel different now? I'm certain it's not only your heart that has changed — look at yourself again in my eyes and you'll know: love has triumphed!

What a great surprise for them both!.... There, searching the eyes of her beloved songbird, she could no longer find the black-and-yellow little bee she had always been.

—I don't understand! she buzzed, unsettled, looking all around, unable to believe what she was seeing.

He, for his part, grateful for what was happening, asked her: —Precious little bee, or hard-working little songbird?! Let's go back to the hive, your Queen must be worried sick.

They flew together, moving as one; she still not understanding her transformation, for she had lost her stinger, her wings were different, and her buzz had turned into an unmatched song.

Together they arrived at the feast; the bees threatened to attack them — the ill-tempered swarm, angered by the invasion of these two bold birds, surrounded them, warning they'd sting them if they didn't leave.

The Queen Bee came before them and asked forgiveness for her kingdom's hostility, but asked the two to leave, since they were interrupting the ceremony where she recognized every subject by their smile.

The new little songbird, who had long been a bee, looked at her with great respect but…. instead of smiling at her, as was their custom, she sang so beautifully that the Queen recognized, in that song, the smile of the little bee no longer among them.

—Oh, God!...Is that you, the bee I've been missing? the Queen Bee asked, like a mother.

—She doesn't understand, and I don't fully understand either — that's why I've brought her before you! the songbird spoke to the Queen with respect, and she said: —There's a prophecy that says love makes us forget our differences, changing all of reality; she was always a bee, but falling in love with you, she wished to stop being what she was, so God transformed her into a songbird.

Then she went on: —I won't stand in the way, I'll let her go, but…. you must love her and respect her forever; if not, you'll be sentenced forever to be a male bee, working eternally for me. And she withdrew to continue the feast.

—Thank you! the two of them answered, grateful.

And they say that ever since, hives strive to build the biggest, sweetest honeycomb, so as to lift each other's spirits, and in turn lift ours; and although the little bee stopped being what she was, they say she never regretted becoming a songbird, and everyone who knows them affirms they work together, brightening the evening, so that with their enchanting song they can lull the Sun to sleep.

Ah…! No one knows whether the fledgling that travels with them will become a Bee one day, or remain a Songbird forever.
