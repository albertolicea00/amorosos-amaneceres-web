The Little Ant Who Searched for a Family

This story aims to strengthen faith, hope, and trust — in God and among people — staying open to searching, to accepting new perspectives, and to facing life's problems honestly in order to solve them. Animal psychology: the ant is hyperactive and hard-working, sturdy in build, and able to carry a weight up to ten times its own.

Once upon a time…… there was a sad, lonely little ant searching for a family, not knowing where to go or who to play with. One day, she looked right, then left, and suddenly saw a crowd of crickets, all together, singing and singing without stopping.

She hurried over and asked if she could come live with them, and the little crickets, looking at her kindly, agreed.

The happy little ant began sharing her life with the hospitable crickets who had welcomed her, but…… after a while, she realized she wasn't like them, and decided to leave.

Along the way, she said to herself: —Oh, my God, I could never live with them!

—What will I do when they have to fly off to warn everyone of bad weather coming?... she wondered as she walked on, worried.

She decided to leave and keep searching!...... What joy — she came across a whole swarm of flies, buzzing about like crazy.

—How lovely! she said to herself. —These will be part of my gang! But…. once she looked closely at what they actually did, at what they fed on, she felt very confused.

She exclaimed: —These flies eat far too much, they only like hanging around where the pig lives, buzzing over the filth and mess in the pen, happy with every kind of garbage…. Shouting a shuddering "Noooooo!", she ran off, looking here, there, everywhere. Weighing and judging what every kind of little animal did.

She searched among the pack of dogs. Found nothing! She searched among the group of cats. Found nothing! She searched among the lizards. Found nothing! She searched among the frogs. Found nothing! And she searched and searched, even among the pride of lions, and....... how sad,…………………….. found nothing!

The little ant was left all alone, by a river, no longer intending to keep searching for a family, crying where the lions and the other forest animals came to drink, grieving with no comfort.

…...At that moment, an eagle saw her from high in the sky. It flew down to where she was and asked why she was so sad.

She answered through tears: —Foolishly, I've been searching for a gang, and no matter how hard I look I can't find anyone, anything that feels or acts like me. (Wiping her nose, she went on between sobs). —That's why I'm tired, and I've stayed here alone, waiting for someone to come find me…..

The eagle looked at her deeply, smiled, and said: —Please, "precious one", dry those tears, because far away there are hundreds of little animals who, just like you, are hard-working, devoted to family to the utmost, who help one another and always travel joyful and united. (It went on, after drying a tear rolling down her face) —If you look at your little face in the water, you'll be able to tell me who you resemble; the day you finally know who they are, you'll have found what you were looking for — but don't take too long to discover it. And it flew off.

The little ant, thoughtful, her tears gone now, laughed with relief. She ran to where the little animals the eagle had pointed to were, and on arriving, happy but timid, she joined the group.

They accepted her, joyful and almost certain they'd known her forever, showing her a rich block of sugar candy they all had to carry together to store for the winter that was almost upon them.

For the first time in her life, the little ant felt the possibility — and the joy — of being with those who shared her own reality.

And they say that ever since, this little ant who searched and searched lived very happily with the family that had always been hers, which, thanks to the eagle, she finally found.

Good night!
Sweet dreams!
