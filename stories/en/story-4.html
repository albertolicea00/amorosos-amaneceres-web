Cuosuelij, the Lazy Turtle

This story aims to strengthen solidarity — not only as a willingness to help and to share, but as companionship that counters laziness and human indifference. Animal psychology: slow but persistent; at times edible; said of persuasive individuals.

Once upon a time…… there was a lazy little turtle everyone called "Cuosuelij." Every time the sun came out, this little turtle poked her head out of her shell, looked at the sunrise, and yawned once or twice.

Then she'd go back inside her little house, tidying every space, every line, every crack God had drawn on her, but…. whenever she set out to leave the house and walk a little, she'd tire and yawn until she fell fast asleep.

One day, an old turtle who had watched her being born came to her and said: —Cuosuelij, why do you take so long to answer when I call you? the old turtle asked.

—It's because I work so hard, and every time someone taps on my shell, I mean to answer, uh…. but all that comes out is one long, long yawn from deep inside — the little turtle replied to her wise old friend.

The old turtle advised her: —When the sun comes up again and you go out, as you always do, to give the morning your smile, wait for me — together we'll find a solution to your problem. Then she went on: —Remember, if you see I haven't arrived, don't worry, I'll find you — don't fret!

The lazy little turtle went to sleep happy, hoping to see the sun soon; her workload would ease, her life would blossom in good company, thanks to her friend the old turtle's advice.

When she woke, morning and the birds' singing roused Cuosuelij, ready to give everyone her smile. What joy at seeing her wise friend's face waiting for her! Though her surprise grew even greater seeing what she'd brought as a possible solution to the laziness weighing on her.

The old turtle brought her nothing less than… the great head of a male turtle, far, far lazier than she was, who had turned up homeless, searching for the little house he — so forgetful — had lost, who knows where!

Seeing him, she smiled, lowered her little head, and needed no further explanation, no more words from her advisor: —Could this be the solution to my low spirits and laziness? she wondered, asking them both, innocently.

The old turtle asked her to share her little house with the male turtle, and the little turtle, without a second thought, agreed with a mischievous tilt of her head.

She looked at the little male turtle and told him a forever "Yes," pulled her little head in a bit, and let this forgetful, lazy tenant in — and they say that ever since, laziness doubled among turtles, keeping them from ever walking very fast. From that moment on, their faces are only seen in the mornings, when they poke out and walk; no one knows for certain whether the little face they see belongs to the kind little turtle or to the male turtle who came to keep her company.

They lived together for about a year; but before the new year could bring fresh joy to her little shell, something unexpected happened. One day the old turtle came to visit and noticed the joy and shine her elusive little head usually showed was missing. She said nothing to them, but mentioned to the rest of the old wise turtles, whose calling was to give counsel, that something was going on. Searching for a possible solution to whatever it was, they all gathered: —I propose repainting her with watercolors, said one. —I'm willing to trade my shell for this turtle family's, offered another.

Nothing worked, it was all in vain! When the four wise old turtles returned to check on her, they found a little girl who, just like me right now, cried without being able to stop her tears, pinching her nose, wiping away the ugly salty little drops sliding down her face and troubling her; sighing, and feeling guilty besides for how still her friend Cuosuelij had become — she could no longer dance, nor did her head respond to the kisses it always used to get; she wasn't breathing.

They called an animal doctor by phone to explain the little turtle's condition and assess her illness. He, with his big glasses, told everyone gathered around Cuosuelij's little bed: —I'm sorry, your friend has just passed away, there's nothing we can do — after a deep, sad silence, he went on: —little male and female turtles who live together in a single shell inhabit both land and water; they need to walk, but to feed them you must find a clean container and offer a little fish, a grub, or a well-chopped worm, and if you have none of that, a small piece of bread, without overdoing it, since it's always best to feed them meat; and if you want to bathe her with you, she can't have shampoo, much less soap or bath gel. After a heartfelt pause, he continued, telling them: She passed away, but only you can know what truly affected your pet.

The little girl hugged her mother tightly, stroked Cuosuelij's soft little shell one last time, and, in a customary ritual, laid her in the river running beneath their house.

Saying goodbye to her still, lifeless friend, she quietly asked forgiveness for having fed her cream with a little piece of roast pork for breakfast, and for spraying her, now and then, with the perfume she wore for Sunday strolls.

This little girl learned that perfumes shouldn't be shared even with beloved pets, and that not every food is safe, understanding through this sad event that good intentions don't always lead to good outcomes. She hadn't known how to care for her pet — no one had ever taught her — but what she does know now is that from that very river, another turtle, male or female, will one day be born; it won't be named Cuosuelij, but it will be very, very much like her, or like the male turtle who once lost his home.

And they say that the following day, their fisherman friend managed to catch that very turtle, and she — and all of us, thanks to the doctor's guidance — will know how to care for her, pet her, feed her, and even take her for walks; and what we're all certain of is that everyone can live together, lovingly, inside and outside the very same shell, content within one and the same little house.
