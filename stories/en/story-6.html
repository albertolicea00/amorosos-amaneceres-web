The Caterpillar Who Dreamed of Flying

This story aims to strengthen respect as the foundation of peace, opening the way to freedom and to every possibility of being and doing beyond a poorly wielded authority that seeks to tip the balance of justice. Animal psychology: fleeting, yet even so it enables growth and passage; it applies to the shift from one situation or emotion to another. Said of people capable of changing their tastes often, usually versatile.

Once upon a time…… there was a green caterpillar who felt unsettled in a broken piece of trunk on a tree. This little caterpillar began looking for somewhere to live, until a little lion, correcting her path, led her to his den, making her climb — reluctantly — up a tree where he sometimes rested.

Not wanting to be devoured by this chubby animal's bites, escaping his paws, his snout, and his gaping mouth, the caterpillar climbed up to the split trunk that "Mother Nature" had set aside for her transformation: From caterpillar to chrysalis! From chrysalis to a butterfly that could one day fly!

One morning…. the little caterpillar who had climbed up unsettled began to look different. She was no longer green — now she appeared a dark brownish-black, covered by a membrane dividing her little body into seven pretty segments. Though she could still enjoy life, she no longer moved as before; she stayed perfectly still, as if asleep or frozen before all other living things. Though motionless, looking like "dry wood," she was, for a while, very busy with her transformation into "a caterpillar that could one day fly."

While the grumpy lion wasn't watching, a very bold spider came along, right to where she was, and began weaving a long web that covered the whole opening of the hole in the tree.

Though the caterpillar had grown her two outer eyes, there was nothing she could do about this unwelcome visitor invading her space.

Some time passed…. the spider kept weaving, the little lion kept searching, trying to find where she was — the chrysalis changing her appearance!

The caterpillar hiding within, by nature's whim, now decorated her surroundings with lovely colors: with wings and marvelous antennae, but she needed to perfume the air around her. She woke with a wild longing to fly at last and bring everyone joy with her beauty. But…. how sad!

Opening her little eyes that afternoon, she found herself alone in a hole in the tree with no way out; all dark, sealed off by a spiderweb she couldn't tear free, and every time she tried, the pesky spider would poke her big head out, wanting nothing but to bother her.

The little butterfly, worn out from fluttering, begged the Spider: —Little spider, please! Today I woke up after a long process of sleep, and I need to go out and fly, to perfume my body with the fragrance of the flowers out there. Could you please open the door of this hole?

The bold, impertinent spider dodged her question, until, annoyed by the fluttering the little butterfly kept doing, she exclaimed: —I'm sorry, winged worm! I came here to weave, and you were asleep — now I don't want to move from here.

The little butterfly, tired of fluttering, kept protesting, with a sound that woke the little lion sleeping in his den not far from there. Running to the old tree trunk, he asked: —Who's calling? Who needs help? Why are you calling for help, little butterfly?

He looked up at the spider weaving above, but looking closer he spotted, among the webs, a beautiful little butterfly crying between flutters — and realizing it, he saw it was the caterpillar who had once climbed up reluctantly, who had become a still "dry stick," and who, somehow, now decorated the air with her multicolored wings.

Paying no mind to the spider or the other animals lingering nearby, he smiled at the little butterfly and said: —What's wrong, can't you fly, or don't you want to?

—I'm trapped here, the Spider won't let me out. If you want to help me, find the big, hairy Spider and explain that I could die if I don't visit the flowers, the little butterfly answered.

The angry Lion went looking for the Great Big Spider, a huge, hairy spider four times the size of the impertinent one he'd left weaving in the tree. Greeting Madam Spider, he said: —I've come to explain that, in the tree by my den, a spider is living without permission and bothering a friend of mine; this butterfly has just been born, and the spider's web has sealed off the entrance where she was sleeping — please, do something about it.

—I can't! she replied. Right now I'm busy with my own little spider, who has also just been born, and I can't leave her alone; but please, go to the tree, speak to that spider on my behalf, and if you see she won't listen, correct her — that little butterfly needs to fly.

The little lion ran off and, with a long roar, told the Ugly Spider what the Great Hairy Spider had said, and when she refused to leave, he raised his front paw and, with one swift swipe, tore down the whole web, setting his friend free.

No one ever knew whether he caught up with the spider, since she fled quickly, who knows where!

The little butterfly, timid, poked out her head, looked up at the sky where the new day's dawn made the sun's first rays shine. She flapped her wings, thanking her friend the little lion, and flew on and on from flower to flower, breathing in their fragrance, spreading the beauty of her colors to everyone who saw her.

And they say that ever since, all gratitude is returned in kind, for the little butterfly thanks her friend the lion for saving her life, but…. what some don't know is that one day a handful of butterflies helped and saved a "lioness in love," so that, with their help, she too could fly.
