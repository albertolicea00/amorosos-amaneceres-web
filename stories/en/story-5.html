The Rabbit Who Stopped Hopping

This story aims to strengthen patience, which clothes love and truth and opens the way to fellowship strong enough to outlast exhaustion, weariness, or human suffering. Animal psychology: trusting, agile, light on its feet, and at times haughty or proud.

Once upon a time…. there was a rabbit who had decided never to hop again. Every morning, as usual, he went out to do the day's work. He tended the garden with care, clearing away yesterday's weeds and mess, checked the crops, picked the carrots and vegetables ready to eat, and carried them home, where his wife the rabbit waited for him eagerly.

But he no longer did it hopping, as is only natural, as every single rabbit does. This little rabbit walked with great heaviness, dragging his feet and his tail, and even the sack where he kept or carried the food. When he got home, his wife the doe rabbit welcomed him with great affection, put everything away, and felt a strange sadness in him, since she had hot water ready for his bath, clean clothes, a delicious meal, and a long, long conversation until tiredness and the night wore out his eyes.

And so,… days, weeks, and months went by.

Every morning the rabbit went off to work, enjoying, later in the afternoon, a lovely time together as a family. But this wasn't enough.

Seeing him arrive exhausted, walking, dragging his paws, his tail, and even the sack carrying his little food, the doe rabbit felt great sorrow, confusing her feelings toward him, since without meaning to he had lost the joy that had defined him his whole life; she loved him dearly, but felt a pain deep in her little heart that she could barely bear.

One day, quite sure of herself, she said: —I need to do something for him!

And as soon as morning came… she sought out every animal in the forest: along the way she found the calf, the bull, the cow, and the young steer eating the green grass by the spring, and she also found the owl, still awake; the hummingbird, the horse, the mare, the zebra, the rooster, the hen, the donkey, and the dog family, who were helping look after the cattle on "Figueredo's" farm.

Even the cat leapt down from a rooftop! Everyone listened closely to hear what the doe rabbit wanted to tell them.

After a general murmur, a silence fell that the little rabbit used to tell everyone present: —Friends, please, I need your help solving a problem I have, one that torments me, saddens me, and that I don't know how to solve.

The old steer, who had known her forever, worried, interrupted and readily said she could count on him for anything at all, that whatever she needed, he was there to serve her.

The cow, bull, and young steer family said as one: —We're ready! What do you and your hard-working rabbit need?

The white owl, a little sleepy, slow and lazy, said: —I'm ready for whatever you need!

Joining in, the hummingbird sang as it flew to show its feelings too. It was joined by the cat, the zebra, the donkey, the horse, the mare, the birds…ah!…. and the dogs stayed quiet, wondering what they, too, could do for the worried little rabbit and her lovely family.

Encouraged, she told them: —Friends, my loving husband has, for some time now, been very sad; he can't hop anymore; after a long day's work he drags his little paws, his tail, and even the sack he carries the food in. It's been confirmed his body isn't sick, but it seems the routine isn't sitting well with him.

The cat, who never stays still, offered an idea that made everyone laugh: —I propose, he said, quite seriously. I propose stealing his carrots in the afternoon so he worries, and since he won't have carrots to bring home, he'll run off chasing the thief.

The owl planned some small mischief in the garden to make him work harder — feeling more tired than usual, he'd have to hop home so as not to arrive too late and upset his wife.

The dogs proposed, howling, the idea of talking to him so much, so long, that he'd get dizzy and have to hop, run, or fly. None of them slept, so set were they on the plan.

That first night the rabbit came home sweaty, exhausted, and eager for the new day to arrive so he could find out who had stolen his carrots — the ones left behind were all half-nibbled.

When the rabbit reached the garden that morning, he was baffled to see the mess made overnight in his crops: —What a nuisance! How am I supposed to gather all these scattered stones, ruined plants, and branches piled up like a mountain of mess, all by myself?

Determined, he did what he could for his garden, running home in the late afternoon-evening, where his beloved little rabbit waited impatiently.

When he arrived, he jumped in surprise! The dogs, together with their friend the donkey, sitting in the big armchair facing the fireplace, were waiting for him.

Arriving and seeing them there, he greeted the doe rabbit, and…. they didn't even let him bathe — they raised a toast with melon juice, a delicious carrot cake, and amid laughter and chatter, the two of them happily attended to their visitors.

That night's games and conversation kept the rabbit from going to the garden the next morning. They woke up more in love than ever, almost at midday the following day, when a loud but uncertain knock at the door woke them.

Opening the door: the cat, the owl, the hummingbird, and the zebra…. arrived singing, carrying the carrots they had mischievously stolen — to help him.

Seeing the pile of vegetables gathered in his little house, and the tidy sum of money it would bring him, he took his wife the doe rabbit by her front little paws, and they hopped in circles, laughing and crying, until they collapsed to the floor.

When they stood up, in front of their friends, the loving husband looked steadily at his wife, embraced her, thanking her with a beautiful smile — thanking her, his friends, and even dear God for having helped him; for he had noticed a wholesome conspiracy behind it all, meant simply to help him find his lost joy again.

And they say that ever since, rabbits are never seen quite so alone; they always keep each other company, along with the other little animals who remained their friends, and this rabbit family is now joined by a whole crowd of little bunnies, the fruit of a lovely, well-tended love.

Ah... but if along the way you notice a little rabbit hiding, fearful in your presence or in front of another friend it doesn't yet know, it surely never learned what happened to this hard-working, long-eared rabbit; it needs to know that rabbits, though very shy, if they lean on love, can always hop — accompanied, and with so, so, so much joy.
