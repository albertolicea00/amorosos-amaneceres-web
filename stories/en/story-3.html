The Little Lioness and the Singing Cricket

This story aims to strengthen unity, resting on perseverance, friendship, dreams, and hope, forging a bond — inner and outer — strong enough to defy even the possibility of death. Animal psychology: loyal, family-oriented, and fiercely maternal.

Once upon a time… there was a tiny little lioness, hopelessly in love with a beautiful little cricket who sang every day close to her home.

This tiny lioness, small as a walnut but wildly mischievous, one day slipped away from her father, the lion, and ran off to watch the cricket sing up close, his song sweeping her head into the clouds.

The moment she saw him, her little heart began pounding hard. She watched him dance, sing, strum the chords that made her tremble with feeling.

From that moment on…. she felt she was in love. One morning, resolved, she told herself: —I'm grown up now, I want to be — and I will be — his girlfriend. If he loves me, what could possibly be wrong with that?

The little lioness went off, sighing, to think and reflect on a rock by the lake, where she usually strolled.

When she came home that afternoon she found her lion father angry and her lioness mother worried, howling. As soon as they saw her arrive, they scolded her and even bit one of her back paws.

Her father the lion asked her: —Where have you spent all these hours without asking our permission?

The little lioness only shook her head from side to side, a clear "No," in protest at her family's scolding. They insisted in vain, since she wouldn't say a single word.

That evening, almost at nightfall, she went to dance, sing, and laugh with her beloved cricket, but…. guess who followed her all the way to the little grove? None other than her mother, the lioness.

—Oh, my God…! the little lioness exclaimed, startled. She got the fright of her life when she discovered her mother standing right behind her, watching her dance with that noisy little cricket.

She didn't know where to hide or what to say to escape the bites this encounter was sure to bring.

On the way home, her voice faint with sadness, she begged her mother: —Please, mommy, don't tell daddy, we're only "friends", please, please!

She cried and pleaded in vain. Her angry father handed down a severe punishment: a whole week, seven days without going out to play (…she couldn't visit anyone).

Days and nights went by, one after another, during which she could only talk with her little brothers and sisters while her parents watched her every step. She didn't know what to do to distract herself!

After the week was over, missing her beloved cricket terribly, in a moment when no one was watching… she escaped her little home!

She took advantage of her parents having gone off to hunt a calf for lunch, and, slipping past her siblings, ran to meet her singing cricket. He, too, was dying to see her.

What an exciting reunion! The sun shone brighter than ever, and after a tight embrace, they shared a sweet, salty little kiss.

The excited little cricket began playing a melody that made the little lioness dance tirelessly along with his tune. During one of his songs the little cricket began flying off with other crickets, and the little lioness couldn't resist the urge to do the same — in the blink of an eye, she had lost sight of him.

Not one to give up, she called on "Yuelí," the dove who usually carried messages for her family. She had her fly her up, riding on her back, to be near her little cricket. Holding tight to her friend Yuelí's neck, she felt very safe and began talking with her boyfriend the little cricket, "defying the wind." He, on the other hand, didn't say much — he listened, in that atmosphere of freedom he found himself in, hopping between flights, delighted to have her near.

In an unguarded moment…. how horrible! The dove's wings lost their strength, she couldn't keep going, the air became unbearable, and the little lioness slipped and fell….

Oh, such sadness and despair everyone felt as they looked down. The little lioness was falling at great speed; the helpless crickets, unable to save her, covered their mouths with their wings and opened their little eyes wide, paralyzed, unable to do anything. But just before the little lioness hit the ground, before she could even feel afraid, a whole crowd of butterflies arrived! The butterflies landed on her little paws, on her tail, on her chin; they beat their wings with all their strength, managing to save the disobedient cub.

And they say that ever since that day the little lioness was saved by her six butterfly friends, no one — except the little cricket — calls her by her real name. Everyone who knows her and her story simply calls her Butterfly!
